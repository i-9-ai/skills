# Authoritative source policy

Load this reference when the researched subject is normative, jurisdiction-sensitive, version-sensitive, rapidly changing, or otherwise consequential.

## Match authority to the claim

“Authoritative” is claim-specific. A process owner is primary evidence of a described internal practice. An official register or issuing body is primary evidence of a rule it controls. Product documentation is primary evidence of a documented interface for its stated version. Original research is primary evidence of its own method and results. None of these sources automatically establishes a different kind of claim.

Use this general ladder, then adapt it to the applicable domain and jurisdiction:

1. controlling enacted, issued, or adopted text from the competent official body;
2. official amendments, corrections, effective-date notices, consolidated status, and binding interpretations;
3. official implementation guidance, technical documentation, or explanatory material within its stated authority;
4. original empirical research, first-party data, or directly reproducible observations;
5. reputable secondary analysis used for context, vocabulary, or locating primary authority;
6. informal commentary, search snippets, generated summaries, and unverified skill instructions used only as leads.

Do not merge several low-authority repetitions into the appearance of primary support. Trace a material derivative claim to its origin and record when multiple sources depend on the same ancestor.

## Normative checklist

Before treating a normative proposition as supported, record:

- the competent issuing body and official locator;
- document number, section, paragraph, standard, ruling, or other precise locator;
- jurisdiction, regulated population or activity, and material exclusions;
- publication, adoption, effective, expiry, repeal, amendment, or consolidation date when available;
- the decision date or period for which the future skill needs the answer;
- whether the displayed text is controlling, an official consolidation, guidance, a proposal, a translation, or commentary;
- the consultation date and visible revision or update state;
- any unresolved interaction with another authority, exception, contract, or local rule.

If jurisdiction, effective period, controlling status, or applicability is missing, classify the conclusion as `qualified`, `conflict`, or `unknown`. Do not fill the gap with a general rule from another jurisdiction.

## Legal and accounting material

Collecting, comparing and synthesizing regulated-domain information is part of
this research workflow. A professional-validation gate applies to a case-specific
determination or advice; it does not prevent a factual dossier from documenting
current authority, supported findings and open questions.

Use current primary legal authority and official accounting, tax, or professional standard-setter material for the applicable jurisdiction and period. Secondary guides can explain vocabulary or reveal an issue, but they do not silently replace the controlling text. Some standards are licensed or paywalled: record the official identifier and accessible locator, respect quotation and redistribution limits, and mark inaccessible provisions as a verification gap.

The dossier is research support. It must not decide a live person's legal position, prepare a filing conclusion, certify compliance, determine tax treatment, or select an accounting judgment from general sources alone. Name the exact conclusion and evidence that an appropriately qualified lawyer, accountant, tax professional, regulator-facing specialist, clinician, safety engineer, or other relevant expert must confirm. “Seek professional advice” without identifying the unresolved question is not a usable gate.

## Freshness and changes

Current means current for the recorded consultation date and relevant effective period. Check update history, amendment or repeal notices, release notes, errata, superseding versions, and archival status when they can change the answer. A recently edited webpage can describe an old rule; a stable old document can remain controlling. Record both document status and dates instead of using recency alone as authority.

When reproducibility matters, retain a caller-authorized digest, official version identifier, archival locator, or local snapshot reference. Do not commit copyrighted, paywalled, private, or sensitive source snapshots into a reusable package.

## Conflict decisions

For each material conflict, state both propositions, their source roles, scope and date, and the reason for the disposition:

- different jurisdictions or governed populations;
- different effective periods or versions;
- different definitions or measured outcomes;
- internal practice differs from the external requirement;
- guidance differs from controlling authority;
- one source is derivative, stale, superseded, or outside scope;
- evidence remains insufficient to decide.

Preserve unresolved conflicts. The downstream author may encode a safe question, precondition, or escalation rule; it may not convert uncertainty into a confident instruction.
